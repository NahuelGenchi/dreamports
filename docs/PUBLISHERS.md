# Working with publishers

[Back to README](../README.md) · [Notices from rights holders](RIGHTS.md) · [The Dreamport standard](STANDARD.md) · [Written in: #584](https://github.com/NahuelGenchi/dreamports/issues/584)

## In plain words

If you hold the rights to a classic game, taking a fan port down isn't your only choice. The Dreamports team would rather make it with you: a faithful, improved version of your game that runs on every device strong enough for it, made under your license and released only with your approval.

To start, write to [hello@dreamports.net](mailto:hello@dreamports.net?subject=Working%20together) with "Working together" in the subject. No rights holder has signed an agreement yet, so this page says what the team offers and how a first conversation goes, not the terms of a deal.

## What a Dreamport is

A Dreamport is the port fans always dreamed of: the game you remember, with the extras you wished it had. It isn't an emulator. The game's own logic is rewritten as ordinary code, so it runs natively on phones, computers, tablets and TVs, wherever the hardware is good enough, and keeps running as new devices arrive.

Every Dreamport is checked against the original. The [proof tools](../tools/README.md) play the same button presses in both and compare every frame and its sound, so with the extras switched off a Dreamport plays exactly like the game on its console. Every change is read by the Dreamports team before it goes live, and the [standard](STANDARD.md) every Dreamport keeps is public.

## What the team offers

A modern release of a game that has none, or a better one than it has, with the proof that it's faithful. The work is done in the open by a community of contributors and their coding agents, and the team reviews all of it. You decide whether your game is made at all, which extras it gets, when it goes live and how it's credited, and you can ask for it to be removed at any time.

## What a license could change

Today every Dreamport needs the player's own copy of the game, and nothing from the game is ever shared, as [the standard](STANDARD.md#nothing-from-the-game-is-ever-shared) says. With your license, a Dreamport could go further. It could carry your game's own graphics and sound so players need no copy, link to where you sell the game, appear as an official version under your name, or be built or reviewed together with your own developers.

Which of these apply, and any payment either way, is agreed with each rights holder in writing. The standard will say how a licensed Dreamport differs once the first agreement exists. Until then nothing changes for games made without a license.

## How to get in touch

Email [hello@dreamports.net](mailto:hello@dreamports.net?subject=Working%20together) with "Working together" in the subject. Say which game or games you mean, who you are or whom you act for, and what you have in mind. If you aren't sure you hold the rights, say so, and the team will help work it out. The address forwards to the Dreamports team.

## What happens next

1. **The team answers within a business day**, to say your message has arrived and ask for anything missing.
2. **You talk it over**, by email or a call: what you'd like, what the team can do, and what a first version would look like.
3. **You agree in writing** on what the Dreamport may use, how it's credited, any payment, and how the agreement can end.
4. **It becomes public only when you say so.** GitHub issues are public, so nothing about the conversation appears there or on dreamports.net until you agree to announce it.
5. **It's built in the open and you approve it.** The Dreamport is made like any other, you see each preview, and it reaches dreamports.net only once you approve it.

## If a Dreamport of your game already exists

You can approve it as it is, make it official, take part in it, or ask for it to be removed. [Notices from rights holders](RIGHTS.md) explains how a removal works, and the team does it within a business day, whatever else you've talked about.

## What stays the same

Dreamports stays an unofficial fan project. Working with one rights holder doesn't mean any other endorses it, and the site still never uses publishers' logos, box art or characters in its own design. Games made without a license keep every rule of the standard.
