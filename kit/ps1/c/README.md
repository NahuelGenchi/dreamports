# The PlayStation's kit in C

[Back to README](../../../README.md) · [The kits in C](../../c/README.md) · [Other consoles](../../../docs/CONSOLES.md) · [Written in: #1558](https://github.com/NahuelGenchi/dreamports/issues/1558)

A PlayStation game in C runs on this kit, on top of what every console's kit in C shares ([`kit/c/`](../../c/README.md)). It writes the console's graphics chip, the GPU, as its original does, a word at a time to its two ports, and the kit draws what it's told into the GPU's own video memory and shows the part the GPU is told to show. It draws with psx_gpu, the GPU of the emulator `prove` compares a Dreamport with, PCSX ReARMed, so what a game draws comes out as the emulator draws it, pixel for pixel. It writes the sound chip, the SPU, as its original does too, a halfword at a time to its registers, and moves its sounds into the chip's memory as DMA does, and the kit mixes them with dfsound, the same emulator's sound chip, so what a game plays comes out as the emulator mixes it. It reads the pads through their serial port, a byte at a time, and the kit's pads answer as the emulator's do. Like the rest of the kits it's under [GPL-3.0-or-later](../LICENSE), psx_gpu's and dfsound's files with it, and nothing from any game is in it.

## What's in it

## Writing a game on it

A game defines `game_start()`, run once, `game_step(buttons)`, run every frame, `game_draw()`, which calls `gpu_show()`, and `game_sound()`, as on every console ([the kits in C](../../c/README.md)). Where its original writes a word to GP0, at `0x1F801810`, the game calls `gpu_gp0(word)`, and where it writes GP1, at `0x1F801814`, `gpu_gp1(word)`, with the same words: so a Dreamport's GPU work is its original's, word for word. `gpu_status()` gives what a read of GP1 gives, and `gpu_vram[y][x]` is the GPU's 1 MB of video memory, 1024 by 512 pixels of 15-bit colour, red lowest. Where its original writes the sound chip's registers, from `0x1F801C00` (`SPU_BASE`), the game calls `spu_write(address, value)` with the same address and halfword, and `spu_read(address)` where it reads one. Where its original moves sounds into the chip's memory through DMA's channel 4, after setting the chip's transfer address as the original does, it calls `spu_dma_write(halves, count)`, and `spu_dma_read` the other way. Its `game_sound` calls `spu_sound(left, right, count)`, the chip's samples. The chip keeps time by the samples the page has heard, as a Dreamport has no processor's clock to keep it by: what a game writes in a frame is heard from where the frame's sound starts, as an original's writes are when it makes them just after the V-blank, which is where most games' sound drivers run. `npm run dp -- new <game>/c` writes a game that sets up a screen of 320 by 240 and fills it, and hears the chip. Where its original talks to the pads through their serial port, SIO0, from `0x1F801040` (`SIO_BASE`), it calls `sio_write(address, value)` and `sio_read(address)` with the same addresses and values, waiting for a byte to come back as its original does, which it has at once; where its original reads the buttons the BIOS keeps for it, `pad_buttons(port)` gives the same 16 bits.

## What the GPU draws so far

- **GP0**, drawn by psx_gpu: a rectangle of video memory filled (`02`); triangles and quads, flat or shaded (`20` to `3F`), lines and lines through many points (`40` to `5F`), and rectangles of their own size and of 1, 8 and 16 pixels square (`60` to `7F`), each plain or textured, with textures of 4 and 8 bits through their colour tables or of 16, a texture window (`E2`), the console's four ways of mixing a colour with what's under it, and its dithering (`E1`); the drawing area they stay inside (`E3`, `E4`) and the offset they're moved by (`E5`); and the mask bit, set and checked (`E6`). A textured quad that lines up with the screen and its texture is drawn as a rectangle, as the emulator draws it.
- **GP0**, moved by the kit: pictures moved into video memory (`A0`) and within it (`80`), as the console moves them, with the mask bit.
- **GP1**: reset, the display on and off, where in video memory the screen starts, its mode, 256, 320, 368, 512 or 640 across, and as many lines as its vertical range, twice them when it interlaces 480.

## What the sound chip does so far

What dfsound mixes, as it mixes it: 24 voices of the chip's ADPCM, each with its envelope, its two volumes and its pitch, a voice's pitch moved by the one before it, noise, the reverb, and the master volume; sounds moved into the chip's memory by DMA or written a halfword at a time, and read back; and the chip's status. It mixes with the chip's own Gaussian table between a voice's samples, and with the reverb, as `tools/consoles.mjs` sets the emulator, and it starts as the emulator's BIOS leaves the chip when it starts a game. Where dfsound is further from the console, a Dreamport is too, as `prove` holds it to the emulator: a volume that sweeps is set once, half up or down, where the console moves it a step at a time.

## What the pads do so far

The player's pad, in the first port, is a digital one, as the tools' emulator plugs in: it answers a read, `0x01` and then `0x42`, or `0x43` as the emulator takes it, with `0x41`, `0x5A` and its buttons' 16 bits, low byte first, a bit clear for each button held, L3 and R3 too, as a DualShock does in its digital mode, which is what the emulator answers. The second port has a pad with nothing held. A game hears each frame's buttons from the frame's start, so one that reads them just after the V-blank, as the emulator gives them, says `keysAfterVBlank` in its `original.json` ([Proving](../../../tools/README.md#proving)).

## What it doesn't do yet

The screen in 24-bit colour, a screen that interlaces drawing only its own field's lines, the CD drive, the memory cards, a DualShock's sticks and its analog mode, the BIOS's own pad buffers and the geometry processor aren't here yet, and neither is a picture read back out of video memory through GP0 (`C0`): a Dreamport reads `gpu_vram` itself. The CD's own sound, which the chip mixes in, comes with the CD drive, and the chip's interrupt doesn't reach the processor: a game reads it in the chip's status (`spu_read(SPU_BASE + 0x1AE)`).

## How it's checked

`npm run test:ps1-kit` builds the console's real emulator, PCSX ReARMed, runs a small program of the project's own on it, a background and a square moving two pixels a frame, written in the assembly the tools assemble (`tools/test/made-up-ps1.mjs`), and runs its Dreamport in C on this kit (`tools/test/ps1-kit/main.c`). `prove` finds all 300 frames the same, every pixel and the sound, silence. The square a shade off differs at the first frame, and so does what `new` writes. A second program of its own, `madeUpDrawExe`, draws all of the GPU's drawing above each frame, moved by a drawing offset that changes with the frame, and its Dreamport in C (`tools/test/ps1-kit/draw.c`) is the same in all 300 frames too, and differs at the first once one triangle's corner is a pixel off. A third, `madeUpSoundExe`, plays the sound chip: a tone and a square moved into its memory by DMA, a click written a halfword at a time, and six voices with their envelopes, noise, a pitch moved by another voice's and the reverb, keyed on and off, bent, swept and turned down over 300 frames. Its Dreamport in C (`tools/test/ps1-kit/sound.c`) sounds the same in all 300, by `prove`'s rule for sound, and is found by its sound where it leaves its voice of noise unkeyed. A fourth, `madeUpPadExe`, reads the pad in the first port through SIO0 each frame and draws each byte that comes back and each button's bit, with a recording that holds each button in turn, the stick's four too, and then four at once. Its Dreamport in C (`tools/test/ps1-kit/pad.c`) is the same in all 300 frames, and when it reads the second port, where nothing is held, it differs at the first button held. It runs on every pull request.
